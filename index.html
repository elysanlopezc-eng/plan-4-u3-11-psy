Phase 1

Retrieval Practice & Core Theoretical Frameworks

Time: ~20 Minutes

1. Retrieval Practice: Rapid Knowledge Check (5 mins)

Objective: Re-activate foundational knowledge regarding personality definitions and traits from Plans 1-3.

Quick Classroom Challenge:

"What is the difference between measuring measurable personality traits (like the Big Five) vs. explaining the hidden internal drives that motivate behavior?"

2. Direct Instruction: Two Contrasting Approaches (15 mins)

A. Freud’s Psychodynamic Approach (Unconscious Drives)

Id (Pleasure Principle)

Innate, unconscious biological drives seeking immediate gratification. Unconcerned with societal rules.

Ego (Reality Principle)

The conscious mediator. Balances the unrealistic demands of the Id with the moral constraints of the Superego.

Superego (Moral Conscience)

Internalized societal and parental morals. Strives for perfection and instills feelings of guilt.

Iceberg Analogy: Early childhood experiences shape unconscious conflicts that drive conscious behavior.

B. Humanistic Perspective: Rogers & Maslow (Self-Concept & Growth)

Carl Rogers: Self-Concept & Incongruence

Focuses on subjective experience. Personality is shaped by alignment between:

  • Real Self: Who you actually are right now.
  • Ideal Self: Who you wish or strive to become.

Unconditional Positive Regard fosters healthy congruence.

Abraham Maslow: Self-Actualization

Humans are inherently driven toward personal growth and fulfilling their ultimate potential.

Personality evolves as lower-level biological & emotional needs are satisfied in the Hierarchy of Needs.

Phase 2

Individual Analysis: Interpreting Fictional Character Behavior

Time: ~15 Minutes

Instructions: Read the fictional character vignettes below. Analyze how a Psychodynamic Psychologist vs. a Humanistic Psychologist would explain the root cause of the character's behavior. Click to check key insights.

Character Profile #1: Daniel

"Daniel works 80 hours a week as a perfectionist manager. He constantly fears failing, refuses to delegate tasks, and feels intense guilt whenever he takes a break, despite being extremely successful."

Psychodynamic View: Daniel has an overly punitive, harsh Superego driving unconscious guilt stemming from early childhood pressure to earn perfection.

Humanistic View: Daniel suffers from incongruence; he operates under conditional worth, believing he is only worthy of love when achieving flawless results.

Character Profile #2: Clara

"Clara abandoned a lucrative corporate career to move to a small town and open a community art workshop. She feels deeply aligned with her true values for the first time in her life."

Humanistic View: Clara is pursuing Self-Actualization (Maslow) and achieving congruence between her Real Self and Ideal Self (Rogers).

Psychodynamic View: Clara is using Sublimation (a mature defense mechanism) to redirect repressed unconscious creative impulses into socially productive artistic work.

Phase 3

Cooperative Paradigm Contrast Workshop

Time: ~15 Minutes

Team Challenge: Psychodynamic vs. Humanistic Comparison Matrix

In teams of 4, assign roles (Leader, Recorder, Timekeeper, Spokesperson). Compare the two major paradigms in your notebooks using the structured dimensions below.

Analytical Dimension Psychodynamic Approach (Freud) Humanistic Approach (Rogers / Maslow)
Core View of Human Nature Driven by unconscious biological impulses & conflict. Inherently good, growth-oriented, and self-determining.
Primary Determinant of Behavior Unconscious motives & early childhood experiences. Conscious self-concept & desire for personal growth.
View of Free Will Deterministic (behavior governed by hidden forces). High Free Will (individuals choose their self-direction).
Ultimate Goal of Personality Bring unconscious conflicts into conscious awareness. Achieve Congruence and Self-Actualization.
Phase 4

Plenary Quiz, 5-Min Exit Ticket & Assessment

Time: ~10 Minutes

Interactive Plenary Trivia

Question: According to Carl Rogers, what happens when there is a large gap between a person's "Real Self" and their "Ideal Self"?

5-Minute Exit Ticket (Individual Closure)

On a physical slip or digital exit note, complete the following prompt:

"One key SIMILARITY between Psychodynamic and Humanistic theories is [______], but one major DIFFERENCE is that Psychodynamic theory focuses on [______] while Humanistic theory emphasizes [______]."

Plenary & Exit Ticket Evaluation Rubric

Summative Checklist
Criteria Excellent (4 pts) Satisfactory (3 pts) Needs Work (2 pts) Check
1. Paradigm Mastery Accurately explains both Freud's unconscious models and Rogers/Maslow's growth models. Explains models with minor conceptual vagueness. Confuses psychodynamic and humanistic terms.
2. Case Application Provides compelling dual theoretical explanations for fictional character cases. Applies one perspective clearly, but struggles with the other. Unable to apply theoretical concepts to case studies.
3. Exit Ticket Closure Clearly articulates key similarity and fundamental difference on exit ticket. Identifies difference but misses similarity. Incomplete or inaccurate exit ticket response.
